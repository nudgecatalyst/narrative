Part one · The ideas01 to 04
Balance wheel and hairspring
The insight
Two currencies, not oneBeing admired and being known turn out to be almost unrelated.
The finding
Score a hundred well-known watches on craft and on public recognition. The two barely move together. The relationship measures 0.17, which is close to none at all.
In practice
  • Cartier: recognition 9, craft standing 6.
  • Grand Seiko: craft standing 10, recognition 4.
  • Neither is failing. They are spending different currencies.
Why it matters
Awareness is bought with reach. Esteem is earned with proof. Most brand budgets are spent as if one converts into the other.
Ask yourself
Which of the two are you actually short of, and are you spending against that one or the one that is easier to measure?

Gear train
The trivia
Rolex supplies its rivalsThe industry’s supply map contradicts its marketing map, completely.
The fact
Kenissi was created by Tudor, Rolex’s sibling. Rolex holds a stake. Chanel owns twenty per cent. It builds movements for Tudor, Breitling, Chanel, TAG Heuer, Norqain and Fortis.
Three more
  • Zenith made the engine in the Rolex Daytona from 1988 to 2000.
  • The Omega that reached the Moon ran a movement by Lemania, which also supplied Patek Philippe.
  • Jaeger-LeCoultre spent the last century supplying Patek, Vacheron, AP and Cartier.
The lesson
Almost every industry has a supply map that contradicts its marketing map. Finding it is the fastest way to see who really holds the power.

Equal-arm balance
The tool
The GapOne number that tells you whether you are buying the thing or the signal.
How it works
Score anything twice. Once on what it is: how well made, how hard to make, whether the design mattered. Once on what it says about you: recognition, price, scarcity. Subtract the first from the second.
Reading it
  • Large positive: you are paying mostly for the signal.
  • Large negative: far more object than anyone will notice.
  • Neither is wrong. You should simply know which you are doing.
Works on
A car, a handbag, a school, a job title, a conference, a consultant. The interesting money usually sits in the negative column.
See it run on real watches
Palm frond
The campaign
Make the trophyChopard does not sponsor Cannes. It manufactures the Palme d’Or.
The move
Since 1998 the award every film-maker wants has been made by hand in a Swiss watch workshop. Every year, the most photographed moment in world cinema is a person holding a Chopard.
Why it holds
A sponsor can be outbid. A logo on a backdrop belongs to whoever pays more next year. Nobody can outbid the maker of the trophy.
Same pattern
  • Omega became the watch NASA chose after blind testing, not the watch advertising near astronauts.
  • Van Cleef & Arpels built a school of jewellery arts with four campuses.
  • Slow, expensive, permanent, and impossible to copy in a hurry.
Ask yourself
What is the object everyone in your category wants to be photographed with, and could you be the one who makes it?

Part two · The objects and the people05 to 08
Movement plate and bridges
The story
Forty-two years is not an endingA man of sixty-six restarted a company that had been dead since before he was thirty.
What happened
Founded in Glashütte in 1845. The war flattened the workshops and in 1948 the Soviet administration expropriated the company. The name disappeared.
The return
Walter Lange, the founder’s great-grandson, fled west. In 1990, months after the Wall came down, he went back and refounded it. He was sixty-six. Today the house is regarded by watchmakers as roughly the finest in the world.
The lesson
What survived those forty-two years was not machinery or drawings. It was a set of principles specific enough to rebuild from.
Ask yourself
If your organisation vanished for forty years, what would someone need to start it again, and does that exist anywhere in writing?

Textured dial and hands
The object
Grand Seiko White BirchA nine thousand dollar watch that out-crafts things costing ten times more.
The dial
Textured to look like the birch trees outside the workshop in northern Japan. Not printed. Stamped from a die cut so precisely it catches light the way bark does, never quite the same colour twice in a day.
The hands
Polished by hand until the edges are mirrors. Near a window the markers flash black, because a surface that flat reflects nothing back at you.
The numbers
  • Reference SLGH005. Around $9,000 to $10,000.
  • Craft 9.1 out of 10. Higher than most of the top twenty.
  • Status 4.5 out of 10. One of the widest gaps in the category.
The catch
Nobody at the table will know what it is. Whether that is the flaw or the entire appeal is a question about you, not about the watch.

Bridge with jewelled bearings
The word
“In-house”The industry’s favourite boast, and a smaller deal than the price list claims.
What it means
That the brand built the movement itself rather than buying one. For most of the last century almost everyone bought from the same few Swiss suppliers. When the largest cut off outside customers in 2010, brands had to build their own, then charge for having done so.
What the record shows
Across a hundred widely tracked references, scored from published auction results and secondary-market pricing: in-house averages 7.44, a reworked bought movement 7.26. That difference is noise. Plain bought movements do score lower at 5.70, so the claim is not empty. It is far smaller than the premium attached to it.
Two reminders
  • The Cartier Tank Louis runs a bought movement and nobody has ever cared.
  • A $4,500 Louis Erard on a Sellita base scores 8 with serious collectors, because it borrowed a famous watchmaker’s reputation.
The transfer
Every industry has a vertical-integration boast. If the customer cannot perceive it, it is a cost, not a differentiator.

07Who writes it, and why

Not a watch expert. That is rather the point.

I am Shubho Broto Das. I have spent twenty years building brands, most recently as head of brand and communication for a deep-tech defence company in India, and before that at McKinsey, Essex Lake Group, Ennovent and Xynteo. My work is making complicated things understood by people who have every reason to ignore them.

Mechanical watches are the purest brand-equity business that exists. The metal in one rarely costs a fraction of the price. Everything above that is story, craft and belief, which means the industry is a laboratory for everything I do for a living. I started reading it as research. I kept reading it because it is genuinely wonderful, and because almost every piece of writing about it is designed to make you feel excluded.

Movement is the notebook. Free, monthly, and written for the serious novice rather than the specialist.
